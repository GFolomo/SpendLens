-----------------------------
*** CUSTOMER SEGMENTATION ***
-----------------------------

1) ABOUT THE DATASET
   -----------------
This dataset contains Samples of the online purchase history of 2.4k customers.

* Origin: Kaggle
* License: CCO: Public Domain
* Total Rows: 541,909 (spanning December 1, 2010 to December 9, 2011)
* Total Columns: 8 (InvoiceNo,	StockCode, Description,	Quantity, InvoiceDate, UnitPrice, CustomerID and Country)
* File Size: 23.72 MB (Excel)
* Download link: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/yasserh/customer-segmentation-dataset


2) THE PROJECT 
   -----------
	2.1) Background
	     ----------
Customer segmentation is a crucial strategy in marketing and business intelligence, allowing businesses to divide their customer base into distinct groups based on common characteristics. With the rise of machine learning and data-driven decision-making, businesses can now segment customers more effectively to tailor their marketing campaigns, product offerings, and services.

The dataset can be used to uncover hidden patterns and group customers into segments that share similar habits or preferences. Applying a machine learning technique such as clustering to the dataset can empower businesses to better understand their customer base, improve customer satisfaction, and increase revenue through tailored and targeted strategies.

	2.2 Problem Statement
	    -----------------
Businesses often struggle to understand the diverse needs and preferences of their customers due to the lack of structured segmentation. Without proper segmentation, marketing efforts may be ineffective, customer churn can increase, and opportunities for growth may be missed.

The challenge is to develop a reliable and interpretable machine learning model using historical data to automatically group customers into meaningful segments. These segments could reflect differences in customer demographics, spending behavior/habits, etc. with a goal to help the business optimize its customer relationship strategies.

	2.3) Aim
	     ---
This project aims to understand customers's spending habits through a machine learning model. 

	2.4) Objectives
	     ----------
- Building a Segmentation/Clustering Model using the provided historical data
- Evaluate the Model using Silhouette and Davies-Bouldin Index
- Optimize the Model
- Determine the optimal clusters and their characteristics

	2.5) SCOPE
	    -----
- The study will only cover customers with positive total sales and order values. 
- KMeans Clustering was selected as the model of execution (because it is simple, widely used and can handle large datasets).

	2.6) Methodology
	     ------------
(a) Data Preparation:
Data Loading ---> EDA ---> Feature Engineering ---> EDA---> Data Preprocessing ---> 

(b) Machine Learning
Model Training (Clustering - KMeans) ---> PCA ---> Model Evaluation ---> Model Optimization (Hyperparameter tuning)


- A comprehensive exploratory data analysis (EDA) was carried out to understand the dataset. 
- Null values: the CustomerID column's data type was changed from float64 to int64 and contained 135,080 NAN. These were dropped as this implies that the corresponding sales were done by anonymous customers. By deleting the Null CustomerID entries, 1,454 Null Description entries were dropped too.
- Duplicates: 10,062 of them were detected including first occurrences while the actual duplicates (excluding first occurrences) were 5225 and deleted.
- Feature Engineering: 2 new features were created namely; TotalSales (the product of Quantity & UnitPrice) and TotalOrders (total number of unique invoices)to easily understand the buying behaviors of customers. Further data cleaning was carried out by removing all entries with TotalSales less than or equal to zero (as per scope) while the rest of the features were dropped (deemed irrelevant).
- Data Visualization: from a 2D scatter plot (TotalSales vs TotalOrders), TotalSales values being of larger magnitudes (in thousands) outweighed/dominated over TotalOrders (in tens and hundreds).
- Preprocessing (Scaling): the two created features were therefore standardized because:
		* KMeans is very sensitive to feature scales - It uses Euclidean distance to measure similarity
		* To prevent biased clustering - KMeans might form clusters based on high-magnitude features only
		* To prevent poor performance - KMeans might give meaningless clusters
		* To prevent misleading visualizations - Cluster separation might look random or off
- Model Training: the Elbow method was used to determine the optimal number of clusters
		* Initial value of k: 4
		* The initial scatter plot of clusters was created using scaled features (clusters were seen overlapping)
		* Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was then used at the prospect of visually improving the cluster separation as it reduces noise and projects data into a space where clusters might become more linearly separable
- Model Evaluation: the chosen metrics used were Silhouette and Davies-Bouldin Index. Their values were 0.7676648475771769 and 0.6476538588549042 respectively
- Model Optimization:
		* Manual grid search was used, the best hyperparameters were found to be {'n_clusters': 3, 'init': 'k-means++', 'n_init': 20, 'max_iter': 1}
		* Best silhouette score: 0.8776449463785528 and Best Davies-Bouldin Index: 0.611743172869309

	2.7) RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
	     ----------------------
After hyperparameter-tuning, the optimum clusters were found to be 3 and after applying the PCA technique to the scaled features, clusters became more visually meaningful and overlapping was significantly reduced. A significant improvement in the Silhouette score was noted (0.77 to 0.88). An improved score of 0.88 suggests strong separation and compactness in clusters as Silhouette ranges between -1 and 1. A Davies-Bouldin Index of 0.61 is solid and very low as this index ranges from 0 to infinity, where a lower value is better ( while 0 means perfect clustering). The 3 established customers are:
- Cluster 0: 4209 customers, with minimum and maximum total sales values of below R0.00 and R21,535.90 (AVG of R1,232.71) as well as minimum and maximum total orders of 1 and 27 (avg of 4) respectively.
- Cluster 1: 4 customers, with minimum and maximum total sales values of R132,458.73 and R279,489.02 (AVG of R213,927.10) as well as minimum and maximum total orders of 55 and 248 (AVG of 111) respectively.
- Cluster 2: 109 customers, with minimum and maximum total sales values of R1,296.44 and R123,725.45 (AVG of R20,620.02) as minimum and maximum total orders of 14 and 224 (AVG of 41) respectively.

	2.8) CONCLUSION
	     ----------
After optimizing the KMeans Model and conducting descriptive statistics, the 3 clusters of customers are named as follows for marketing purposes:
- Cluster 0: Low Spenders (including infrequent buyers)
- Cluster 1: High Value Customers (high value and highly frequent buyers)
- Cluster 2: Moderate Spenders (frequent buyers)

With this, the marketing team can effectively design marketing campaigns to better target each segment so as to increase sales/turnovers and improve services. Perhaps attractive discounts should be rolled out at the prospect of converting a portion of low spenders into moderate ones then already moderate ones into high spenders. As for the top spenders, strategic campaigns are to be designed to keep them. 

	2.9) RECOMMENDATIONS
